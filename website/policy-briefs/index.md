# Policy Briefs

Mainstay policy briefs connect practical local-first infrastructure with the
enduring responsibilities of communities and institutions. They are written
for leaders, operators, policy makers, and partners who need to understand the
purpose and implications of the work without beginning with its protocols or
deployment details.

## Clerk and Treasury Functions

Every institutionally sustainable community needs ways to preserve records and
move value. These can be understood as **Clerk** and **Treasury** functions.
The names are functional shorthand, not prescribed offices or governance
models.

Mainstay is not inventing these responsibilities. It is exploring how to
reconstitute age-old institutional capabilities with local-first software,
portable cryptographic identities, verifiable records, private bearer
instruments, and infrastructure that can continue operating when outside
services are unavailable.

## Credit, Clearing, and Transferring Value

Credit relationships are older than coinage and modern banking. Drawing on
David Graeber's *Debt: The First 5,000 Years*, this brief places Clear and the
Treasury function in that longer cultural history.

It distinguishes transferring a claim from clearing the obligation behind it,
then considers how cryptographic signatures, private bearer instruments, and
digital communication can support familiar community practices without
pretending that software creates value, authority, or trust.

## Community-Led Recordkeeping and Digital Originals

A community can establish which exact digital records are authoritative for
work within its own legitimate scope. This brief explains how OpenETR combines
digest-identified artifacts, signed review evidence, consequential state, and
bounded recognition without claiming that a local record replaces its outside
issuer for every purpose.

It also presents graduated disclosure as the practical default for sensitive
records: check first, present when necessary, share only for justified deeper
review, and surrender only when custody or control is meant to change.
